Section 01

Profile Photo

01Profile Photo
5 / 10

Current State  ·  Provisional

A LinkedIn profile export does not include the photo file, so this one has not been inspected. It is scored at the midpoint and the standard below tells you exactly what to check against when you open the live profile.

What to check

Three things decide whether a broker photo works. Is the face large enough to read at 48 pixels, because that is the size it appears at in a feed. Is the expression warm rather than corporate, because borrowers are choosing a person to be honest with about money. And does the background have any colour in it at all, since a grey or white wall makes every headshot look like the same headshot. If the photo is a logo, a group shot, or anything taken before 2020, that alone drops this to a 3.

Shot direction

Shoot outdoors in the hour before sunset with the sun behind him and a brick or shopfront wall six to eight metres back so it blurs out. Frame from mid chest up with the face filling roughly sixty percent of the square crop. Collared shirt, no tie, sleeves rolled. Half smile with the eyes doing the work, looking straight down the lens rather than off to the side. Shoot forty frames and pick the one where he looks like he is about to say something, not the one where he looks composed.

Quick win, today, no photographer

Stand next to a window with the light on his face, get someone to shoot portrait mode on a recent iPhone from about a metre and a half back at eye height, and crop it square with the eyes sitting on the top third line. That beats ninety percent of broker headshots and costs nothing.

Why the banner matters more for a broker than almost any other trade

The banner is the largest piece of real estate on the profile and it is the only part he fully controls as a design. For a broker it does a specific job that the headline cannot, which is to answer "what can you actually get done for me" with something concrete like a loan type, a lender panel size, or a promise about turnaround. A logo on a gradient answers none of that. It is decoration in the one spot that could be doing sales.

Section 03

The Headline

Biggest single gap on the profile, and the fastest thing to fix.

03Headline
2 / 10

Current Headline  ·  Verified

Director at Sunfast Agency

Diagnosis

This is LinkedIn's automatic default. It is what the platform writes for you when you add a job and never change the line. It names his title and his company, both of which matter to him and neither of which matters to a borrower who has never heard of either. There is no product, no buyer, no location and no outcome in it. He has 220 characters available and he is using 28 of them. The headline also follows him everywhere on the platform, so every comment he leaves under a property post is currently introducing him as a director of a company nobody recognises rather than as a broker who solves a specific problem.

Three options. Pick one and paste it.

Helping self employed Australians and investors get the loan the bank said no to | Home, SMSF and commercial finance | Former bank mortgage compliance | [City]

Option A, outcome led. Leads with the result. Strongest for cold search because it opens with the exact situation a declined borrower is in.

Most home loans get declined on structure, not income | I structure yours properly the first time | Mortgage broker for self employed buyers and investors | [City]

Option B, problem led. Strongest for content, because it plants an idea people will argue with in the comments. Best choice if he starts posting.

Former bank mortgage compliance, now a broker | Home, SMSF and commercial lending for self employed Australians | Sunfast Agency

Option C, authority led. Strongest for referral traffic, where someone has already been told about him and is checking he is legitimate.

Why these work, and the one decision he has to make

All three follow the same shape. Who it is for, what changes for them, and one thing that makes him different, in that order. All three put searchable terms in the first sixty characters, which is roughly what shows before LinkedIn truncates the line on mobile. The decision underneath all of this is who he is actually for. His current profile says everyday Australians, which is not a market, it is a population. Self employed borrowers and SMSF investors are the sharpest wedge available to him because those are the files banks decline on structure, which is exactly what he spent a year at the bank looking at. If he would rather lead with first home buyers, swap the phrase "self employed Australians and investors" for "first home buyers in [his suburb or city]" and keep everything else identical.

Section 04

About Section

04About Section
5 / 10

Current opening, summarised  ·  Verified

Opens by naming his own title and the business, states a belief about making finance feel easier, then lists five things he helps with: first home purchases, refinancing, portfolio growth and business expansion, all for “everyday Australians”.

Diagnosis

There is a real person in here, which puts it ahead of most broker profiles. The short, plain spoken lines about no jargon and dealing directly with the decision maker are genuine, and the second one is a genuine advantage. The problem is the shape. It opens with his own title, which is the weakest possible first line because the reader already knows his title from the headline above it. It then lists five product lines, which reads as a menu rather than an answer. "Everyday Australians" tells a self employed borrower nothing about whether he can help with their specific situation, and that borrower is the one with the hardest file and the most reason to pay for expertise. There is not a single number anywhere. No settlement figure, no client count, no approval rate, no lender panel size, nothing a stranger can weigh. And it closes with "let's chat", which is a feeling rather than an instruction. There is no link, no calendar, no keyword, nothing to click.

Rewritten About, ready to paste

Most home loans do not get declined because someone earns too little. They get declined because the file was put together badly. I spent a year inside a major bank reviewing mortgage files for compliance before they went to settlement. I saw the same handful of mistakes over and over. Income structured the wrong way. A credit enquiry nobody bothered to explain. An application sent to a lender who was never going to approve that borrower in the first place. Then I spent the next twenty years running my own businesses, which is where I learned what it actually feels like to walk into a bank holding a tax return instead of a payslip. Now I run Sunfast Agency and I do one thing. I put the file together properly the first time, and I send it to the lender who is actually likely to say yes. That covers home loans, refinancing, SMSF property lending and commercial finance. Most of the people I work with are self employed or buying through a company or a fund, because that is where the structure genuinely matters and where the big four tend to lose interest. In the last [NUMBER] months I have settled [$ AMOUNT] across [NUMBER] clients. [NUMBER] of them had already been knocked back somewhere else. You deal with me the whole way through. There is no processing team and no junior who inherits your file after the first meeting. I run this solo on purpose. If a bank has already said no, or you want someone to check the structure before you apply, book a 15 minute call at [BOOKING LINK] and I will tell you straight whether it is doable.

Four things to fill in before this goes live. The three bracketed numbers and the booking link are deliberately blank. I will not invent settlement figures for a real broker's public profile, and neither should anyone else. If he does not want to publish a dollar figure, the safest swap is a client count and an approval line, for example: "Since opening I have settled loans for [NUMBER] clients, and [NUMBER] of those had already been declined elsewhere." Also check the "twenty years running my own businesses" line against his own view of it. It is drawn from the earliest consulting role through to the two agencies, but he should be the one who signs off on the number.

The formula underneath it

Line one breaks a belief the reader is holding, which is that approval is about income. Lines two through four mirror the exact experience a self employed borrower has already had, so they feel understood before they are sold anything. The bank compliance story is the bridge, because it converts a young brokerage into twenty years of relevant credibility. The numbers make it weighable. The solo line turns his smallest weakness into the reason to choose him. And the close gives one specific action with a time cost attached, which is far easier to say yes to than "let's chat".

Section 05

Featured Section

05Featured
2 / 10

Current State  ·  Provisional

Featured items do not appear in a profile export. Scored at 2 because no lead magnet, calculator, guide or booking link is referenced anywhere in the About section or the work history, which usually means nothing is pinned. Confirm on the live profile.

Diagnosis

The Featured section is the only part of a LinkedIn profile that can capture someone who is interested but not ready. A borrower who is nine months out from buying will never book a call, but they will download something. Without a Featured row, every one of those visitors leaves with nothing and he has no way of knowing they were ever there. For a broker this is the difference between a profile that generates enquiries and a profile that generates a pipeline.

Three slots, in this order

SLOT 1 | Lead magnet, PDF Title: The 9 Reasons Self Employed Borrowers Get Declined (And How to Fix Each One Before You Apply) Why: names the exact fear, promises a fix, and only the right person clicks it. Eight to ten pages is plenty. He can write it from memory in an afternoon because it is literally what he reviewed at the bank. SLOT 2 | Proof, pinned post Title: Knocked back by two banks. Approved in eleven days. Why: one real client story, no name, structured as what went wrong, what he changed, what happened. This is the slot that converts referral traffic. SLOT 3 | Booking link Title: Find out in 15 minutes whether your loan is actually doable Why: outcome framed and time bounded. "Book a consultation" is a commitment. "Find out in 15 minutes" is a favour.

How to add it

Edit profile, scroll to Add profile section, choose Recommended, then Add featured. Links pull their own preview image, so make sure whatever the link points at has a decent thumbnail or it will render as a grey box. If the PDF is not written yet, put the booking link in slot one today and move it down when the guide is ready. An empty Featured section scores worse than an imperfect one.

Reviewed residential mortgage files for compliance and quality before they went to settlement, and managed the QA function for the team doing it. This is the year that shaped how I broke. Section 08

Content Activity

08Content Activity
3 / 10

Current State  ·  Provisional

Posting history does not appear in a profile export. Scored at 3 on the basis that nothing in the profile copy references content, a newsletter or a following. Check the Activity tab on the live profile and adjust.

The real point

A fixed profile earns him the people who already found him. Content is what brings people who have never heard of him. For a solo broker in the first two years, the profile is the conversion asset and the posting is the distribution, and neither works without the other. The advantage he has over almost every other broker posting on LinkedIn right now is that he has actual stories. He sat inside a bank and read the files. That is content nobody can copy, and it does not require him to have a marketing opinion about anything.

30 day rhythm, three posts a week

MONDAY | The teardown Take one real reason a file gets declined and explain it in plain English. Structure it as: here is what the borrower thought the problem was, here is what it actually was, here is what to do instead. Twelve of these exist in his head already from the compliance year. Soft close: "If you are not sure how yours is structured, ask me before you apply." WEDNESDAY | The client story One file, no names. What they came in with, what two lenders said, what he changed, what happened. Keep the numbers real and keep them specific, because a rounded number reads as invented. Soft close: none. Let this one just sit there and do its job. FRIDAY | The opinion One thing he thinks the industry gets wrong. Comparison rates. Pre approvals that are not really approvals. Why the cheapest rate is often the wrong loan. This is the post that gets argued with, and arguing is distribution. Soft close: a question, not a pitch. Rules for all three: Write like he talks. The short, plain spoken lines already in his current About are the voice, he just has not used them anywhere else yet. No carousels for the first month. Text only. Get the writing right before adding design. Reply to every comment within the first two hours. That is worth more than the post itself. One month is twelve posts. That is enough to know whether it works.
Section 09

Niche Density

09Niche Density
3 / 10

Current State  ·  Verified

The headline names a company. The About section names "everyday Australians" and then lists five product lines. The Experience section lists the same five again in more detail. The skills include Digital Strategy. The work history shows a marketing CEO role still running. Five different parts of the profile are answering the question "who is this for" and no two of them agree.

Diagnosis

Everyday Australians is not a niche, it is the whole country, and copy written for everyone lands on nobody. The specific cost here is that the hardest and most profitable files, self employed borrowers and SMSF purchases, are exactly the ones where the borrower shops around for someone who obviously specialises. That borrower reads "everyday Australians" and quietly assumes he does simple PAYG loans. Meanwhile the marketing agency role sitting live in his history sends a second, worse signal, which is uncertainty about what he actually does for a living. Neither of these is a copywriting problem. They are a decision problem, and the decision has not been made yet.

Threading one message through the whole profile

Start by picking the buyer. On the evidence available, self employed and business owner borrowers plus SMSF property investors is the strongest available position, because it is the only one where his bank compliance year and his twenty years of running businesses both become directly relevant. Then run the same language through every section in order. The headline names that buyer and the outcome. The banner repeats the outcome and adds a way to contact him. The About section opens on the structure problem those buyers have and closes on a booking link. The Featured row leads with a guide written specifically for them. The Sunfast Agency description puts self employed and SMSF work at the top rather than buried in a list. The top three skills are Mortgage Lending, Loan Structuring and Commercial Lending. And the marketing role gets an end date so nothing contradicts anything else. Five product lines can still be mentioned. They just stop being the headline act.

The 5 second test

Show the profile to someone who has never met him and ask two questions. Who is this person for, and what do they do. If both answers do not come back inside five seconds, the density work is not finished yet.
